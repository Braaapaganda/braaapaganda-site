Braaapaganda Daily: Roczen and Davies Sweep Geelong

Geelong delivers fresh results, Shimoda makes the SX1 podium, and Anstie and Triumph look toward 2027.

November 7, 2026

Saturday’s AUSX Open provides the fresh race news for this edition: Ken Roczen and Cole Davies swept their classes in Geelong, while Jo Shimoda made the SX1 podium. Back in the U.S. offseason picture, a new Anstie interview explains the work behind the racing, and Triumph has detailed its next 450 update.

Roczen and Davies sweep Geelong

Computime’s provisional SX1 round classification gives Roczen all three final wins and the maximum 75 points. Christian Craig finished second overall on 66, with Shimoda third on 54. GateDrop’s November 7 race report also records the sweep and Shimoda’s podium on his 450 Supercross debut.

In SX2, the provisional classification shows Davies winning all three finals for 75 points, ahead of Lux Turner on 64 and Cameron McAdoo on 58.

The timing sheets remain labeled provisional. For the U.S. audience, the immediate story is another strong offseason outing from Roczen and Davies, plus a podium from Shimoda in the bigger class.

Anstie is using AUSX to sort his 2027 Yamaha

Friday’s Racer X interview with Max Anstie gives the Australian racing a clear U.S. purpose. Anstie told Eric Johnson that the races are helping him develop the 2027 ClubMX Yamaha YZ250F, including its different clutch and chassis setup, before the next AMA Supercross season.

He described progress, but also the gap between feeling comfortable on a practice track and finding what works under race pressure. The aim is to solve those problems in November rather than carry them into January or February. His offseason results offer useful context for the 2027 250SX picture, although the championship is still months away.

Triumph details its next 450 update

A Triumph announcement published by Racer X on November 6 outlines the 2027 TF 450-X’s changes after the manufacturer’s first 450 season in AMA Supercross and Pro Motocross. The list includes revised engine calibration, updated cylinder-head components, clutch and shifting changes, and revised KYB suspension settings.

Triumph’s own model page also lists the powertrain and suspension updates. These are the manufacturer’s development claims. Race results will provide the next test as the 450 program heads into 2027.

Sexton watch: no verified decision

The latest named-source development remains Yarrive Konsky’s acknowledgment of talks with Chase Sexton, covered in Friday’s Daily. No completed signing was verified in the sources reviewed for this edition.

Friday’s edition also covered the remaining AUSX rounds’ Triple Crown format, the 2027 WMX schedule and the AMA rulebook follow-up. Those remain background for today rather than new announcements.

Coverage note: Geelong results are attributed to the official timing provider’s provisional classifications and corroborated by race reporting. The Anstie item comes from a published written interview. Triumph’s technical changes are attributed to the manufacturer.
