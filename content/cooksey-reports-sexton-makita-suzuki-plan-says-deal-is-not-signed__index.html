Cooksey Reports Sexton–Makita Suzuki Plan, Says Deal Is Not Signed

Cooksey reports a proposed Sexton–Barcia Suzuki setup with Makita backing, but says signing and bike testing remain outstanding. Here is what is confirmed.

October 25, 2026

October 25, 2026

Cooksey Media is reporting a proposed Suzuki move for Chase Sexton involving a separate Makita-backed rig shared with Justin Barcia. His October 24 video also carries a qualification that belongs at the top of the story: according to Cooksey’s account, Sexton had not signed the contract or ridden the bike.

That makes this a report about a possible arrangement, not confirmation that Sexton has joined Suzuki or that Makita has agreed to a new title-sponsorship deal.

What Cooksey actually reported

In the opening discussion, around 1:50–2:33, Cooksey presents Suzuki as his expected destination for Sexton. He claims a bike and suspension are prepared, while acknowledging that the contract and first ride remain outstanding. He also allows that the outcome could change.

Around 14:46–15:02, Cooksey describes a proposed separate rig for Sexton and Barcia with Makita as title sponsor. These details are his claims. Braaapaganda has not independently verified the proposed sponsor arrangement, equipment preparations or contract status.

Near the end of the video, around 20:17–20:32, he again says signing and riding the bike still need to happen. Those caveats matter more than the confidence of the prediction. A plan can change before a rider signs, tests the motorcycle and publicly confirms the program.

Makita is already involved, but in a different capacity

There is a confirmed Makita connection to Pipes Motorsports Group. The company’s January 5, 2026 announcement describes a technical partnership supplying tools and MAKTRAK storage for the team’s operations.

That existing agreement does not verify Cooksey’s new claim about title sponsorship. Providing technical equipment and putting a sponsor’s name on a proposed race program are different commercial roles. The January release should not be presented as an announcement of a Sexton deal.

Barcia’s move is already official: PMG announced on October 3 that he would join Suzuki for 2027. That confirms Barcia’s place in the program, not the proposed Sexton pairing or shared-rig configuration described by Cooksey.

Why it matters, and what remains open

If accurate, Cooksey’s report would give Sexton a new destination and add a distinct sponsor-backed setup within Suzuki’s racing effort. That would be a significant development in the 2027 team picture. The report does not establish that another rider is being displaced, and no such conclusion should be drawn from it.

In a check at 12:21 p.m. Eastern on October 25, we found no official Sexton-to-Suzuki signing or new Makita title-sponsorship announcement in the Suzuki, PMG and Makita sources checked. The next useful evidence is confirmation from the rider, team or sponsor specifying the agreement and program. Until then, this remains Cooksey’s conditional report.

Source note: We reviewed the video’s available YouTube transcript and the written announcements linked above. We have not independently confirmed the proposed deal.
