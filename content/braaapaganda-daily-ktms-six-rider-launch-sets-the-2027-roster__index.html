Braaapaganda Daily: KTM’s Six-Rider Launch Sets the 2027 Roster

Prado and Lucas Coenen lead the 450 effort, four riders fill the 250 program, and Paris provides the next offseason checkpoint.

November 18, 2026

KTM has turned its 2027 American racing plans into a complete, named factory lineup. That is today’s lead, rather than another round of destination guesses. We also catch up on the Perth results since our last edition and look ahead to Paris, while keeping older developments clearly dated.

Confirmed: two 450 riders, four 250 riders

At its November 17 U.S. launch, Red Bull KTM presented Jorge Prado and Lucas Coenen as its 450 riders, with Julien Beaumer, Sacha Coenen, Ryder DiFrancesco and Daxton Bennick in the 250 program. Ian Harrison leads the 450 operation; Nathan Ramsey manages the 250 effort. The manufacturer’s announcement, dated November 18 in its international press center, covers Supercross, Pro Motocross and SMX.

The unfinished detail is the 250 Supercross split: KTM says East and West assignments will be announced before the season. Lucas’s early-November surgery also remains important context, not proof that his recovery is complete. Anaheim 1 is scheduled for January 9.

Our take: The useful question has shifted from who gets a seat to how this group is deployed. Four 250 riders create several possible coast combinations. Until KTM identifies them, putting particular teammates into an East-versus-West title scenario is premature. A team presentation establishes the roster; racing will establish the pecking order.

Paris: a checkpoint, not an Anaheim verdict

The organizer’s November 17 preview, carried by Racer X, lists Ken Roczen, Jason Anderson, Jo Shimoda, Aaron Plessinger, Jordon Smith and Tom Vialle among the SX1 names for November 21–22. Sacha Coenen is listed for SX2.

Anderson replacing Lucas is not new to this Daily: we covered that change on November 12. The value now is the approaching race itself. Plessinger’s Triumph outing and Sacha’s Supercross appearance offer concrete things to watch, rather than trying to decide an entire American season from offseason training clips. An entry list is still not a guarantee that every listed rider will start.

Since our last edition: Craig and Anstie win Perth

November 14 race; report published November 16. The AUSX organizer’s report confirms Christian Craig won SX1, ahead of Cédric Soubeyras and Austin Politelli. Max Anstie passed Cameron McAdoo on the final lap to win SX2, with Michael Hicks third.

Craig left Perth leading SX1 on 207 points, ahead of Luke Clout’s 163. McAdoo retained the SX2 lead on 174. The series finale is November 28 in Newcastle. Those are Australian championship results, not SMX points, but they provide competitive mileage for riders preparing for the American season.

The media conversation and the next useful answers

Racer X’s KTM and Paris coverage includes clearly labeled press releases. Those are attributable team and organizer statements, not independent corroboration simply because several outlets republish them. Vital MX has also published an Ian Harrison interview whose page lists Sexton rumors and the Coenens among its topics. We have not reviewed a transcript, so we are not assigning Harrison answers based on the headline. Inaccessible video interviews are not evidence for quotes, hidden motives or unannounced deals.

Next up: KTM’s coast assignments, the actual Paris starters and results, and Newcastle’s title deciders. Those developments will tell us more than another speculative depth chart.
