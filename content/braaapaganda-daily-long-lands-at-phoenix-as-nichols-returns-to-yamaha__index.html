Braaapaganda Daily: Long Lands at Phoenix as Nichols Returns to Yamaha

October 28, 2026

October 28, 2026
Research cutoff: 8:29 a.m. EDT / 12:29 UTC.

Two more pieces of the 2027 rider market are settled. Avery Long is heading to Phoenix Racing Honda with a confirmed 250SX East assignment, while Colt Nichols is returning to Yamaha through ROKiT Partzilla PRMX. Both announcements arrived Tuesday, after the cutoff for yesterday’s Daily.

The useful distinction this morning is between confirmed plans and offseason expectations. We have teams, a regional assignment for Long and testing underway for Nichols. What those moves deliver on the track remains to be seen.

Long gets a Honda seat and an East assignment

Phoenix announced Long on October 27, placing him on a CRF250R for the 2027 AMA Supercross, Pro Motocross and SMX championships. Long’s statement specifically identifies 250SX East as his Supercross assignment, and the release names Tampa as his team debut. Those details are in the original Phoenix announcement and the full team release carried by Racer X.

Long described the attraction of returning to Honda, working with Phoenix’s technical staff and being based on the East Coast. His stated objective is to turn the speed he has shown into more consistent results. Phoenix also says more rider announcements are coming.

That gives this signing a practical significance beyond another jersey photo. Regional assignments often remain unresolved when teams announce riders. Long’s is already specified, allowing fans to follow how the East field develops without guessing where Phoenix intends to place him.

The next question is the rest of the lineup. Additional announcements should provide a clearer picture of the group Long will train and race alongside. For now, the confirmed news is his seat and program; consistency is the goal, not an outcome the announcement can establish.

Nichols returns to Yamaha as PRMX changes brands

ROKiT Partzilla PRMX confirmed Nichols on October 27. The team says he will retain No. 45 and has already been testing at its Florida facility. Nichols described the agreement as the result of several months of discussions with team owner Julien Perrier and welcomed a return to a brand with which he previously enjoyed success. See the original team announcement and the team statement published by Supercross Live.

There is a second transition here: the team itself is moving from Kawasaki to Yamaha. MotoOnline’s October 27 reporting places Nichols alongside Mitchell Harrison in the 450 program and says team manager Kraig Riese remains.

Our read: the early testing matters because rider and team both have adaptation work ahead. Nichols’ familiarity with Yamaha is relevant background, but a positive first impression is still a rider assessment. It is too early to turn it into a prediction about finishes.

The media conversation: leading the championship

PulpMX’s Nick Taylor published a fresh historical perspective on October 27: an analysis of premier-class Supercross points leaders covering 1974 through 2026. His count puts Jeremy McGrath at 106 rounds leading the standings, followed by Ryan Dungey at 63 and Ricky Carmichael at 62. Taylor also counts Ken Roczen leading the points in nine different seasons. The methodology and results are in PulpMX’s Super Stats feature.

These are championship-leadership figures, not race-win totals. That distinction makes the piece useful: it offers another way to discuss sustained title contention without confusing a points lead with a victory. The article is newly published; the racing history it examines is older context.

What to watch next

Watch Phoenix’s remaining roster announcements and PRMX’s preparation on Yamaha. Those are concrete follow-ups to confirmed developments. Yesterday’s Cochran and Star Yamaha edition covers the earlier moves; today’s signings add to that picture rather than repeat it.

Source note: Team releases republished by different outlets are treated as one originating announcement, not independent corroboration. No inaccessible video commentary has been presented here as a reviewed transcript.
