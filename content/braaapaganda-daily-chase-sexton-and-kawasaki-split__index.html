Braaapaganda Daily: Chase Sexton and Kawasaki Split

Chase Sexton and Kawasaki confirm their split, Beta renews LIQUI MOLY backing, and AM Open’s current Saturday schedule lists a later program start.

October 24, 2026

October 24, 2026
Research cutoff: 8:31 a.m. Eastern / 12:31 UTC.

The biggest change since Friday’s roundup is Chase Sexton’s departure from Kawasaki. Beta also has renewed title-sponsor backing for its racing program, while this morning’s AM Open schedule deserves a fresh look before anyone relies on yesterday’s timing.

Sexton and Kawasaki make the split official

Kawasaki Racing announced on October 23 that the team and Sexton had mutually agreed to part ways, effective that day. The announcement credited him with 450SX wins at Anaheim and Salt Lake City and wished him well for 2027.

Sexton’s own post thanked Kawasaki and its staff. Neither statement named his next team.

That is the confirmed development. The announcement does not establish the financial terms of the separation or explain every factor behind it. We are not filling those gaps with paddock speculation.

Why it matters: The offseason picture has changed around a proven race winner. The useful next information is a named destination, an official team announcement and the program he will actually race. A familiar logo appearing in a rumor is not enough to settle those questions.

For a text version of the release, Racer X carries Kawasaki’s statement. Its reproduction is the same announcement, not a separate source proving additional claims.

Beta secures continued title backing

Beta USA announced on October 23 that LIQUI MOLY will remain its title sponsor for at least the next two years. The backing includes the 2027 250SX program on the RX 250F and the team’s continuing off-road effort. The Beta-issued release carried by Racer X also confirms the LIQUI MOLY Beta Racing name will continue.

The renewal is the fresh news. The 250SX project was already public: Derek Kelley’s signing was announced September 8, followed by Gage Linville on September 28.

Why it matters: Sponsor continuity is a concrete part of building the program. It is not proof of how competitive the motorcycle will be, and no financial amount was disclosed. Keep the business commitment separate from predictions about results.

AM Open: check today’s start time

Update to Friday’s roundup: The organizer’s current AM Open schedule lists Saturday, October 24 practice and qualifying from 9 a.m. Arizona time, noon Eastern. That differs from the earlier 8 a.m. Saturday listing used in older event information.

The same schedule still labels its broadcast links at 8 a.m. Do not confuse a stream listing with the on-track program start. Use the organizer’s latest schedule and staging calls for race timing.

Saturday’s free InsiderMX coverage is linked here, and the organizer has posted the Saturday qualifier order. Sunday’s listed program remains 8 a.m. Arizona time, or 11 a.m. Eastern. These are scheduled times, not results or a guarantee against further changes.

The media conversation

Swapmoto Live has published a reaction to the Sexton split. Its written introduction distinguishes the official exit from discussion about possible destinations. We reviewed that introduction, not the video transcript, so we are linking the discussion rather than presenting its unseen analysis as reporting.

What to watch next

  • An official announcement resolving Sexton’s next ride, rather than another round of destination guesses.
  • AM Open qualifying results and any further organizer updates before Sunday’s program.
  • More concrete details about Beta’s 250SX preparation as the already-announced project moves toward 2027.

For now, the useful distinction is between confirmed team decisions, sponsorship commitments with undisclosed amounts, and questions that remain open. That is where we will keep the next roundup focused.
