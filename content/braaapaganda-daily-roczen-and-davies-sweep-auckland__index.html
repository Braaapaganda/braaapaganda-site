Braaapaganda Daily: Roczen and Davies Sweep Auckland

October 31, 2026

The latest development in the 2027 Supercross build-up came on a racetrack rather than in a signing announcement. Ken Roczen and Cole Davies swept their respective classes at the Australian Supercross Championship round on October 31. Meanwhile, Joey Savatgy has explained what the Ducati move means beyond a change of motorcycle.

Auckland: three finals, three wins apiece

The official provisional SX1 and SX2 points sheets show that Roczen won all three SX1 finals at North Harbour Stadium. Christian Craig finished second in each, with Luke Clout third each time. In SX2, Davies also went 1-1-1, while Cameron McAdoo took three runner-up finishes.

The event’s official provisional points sheets list Roczen and Davies on 75 points apiece, with Craig and McAdoo on 66. These remain provisional results. Auckland was international offseason racing, not an AMA Supercross or SMX points round.

For American fans, the useful result is repeated race execution from riders expected to feature in the US conversation. Roczen's Suzuki return was already covered in yesterday's Daily; Auckland adds a performance to that contract story. Davies and McAdoo also provide a fresh 250-class comparison. Analysis: this is encouraging offseason evidence, but one event with a different field cannot settle the 2027 AMA title picture.

Savatgy: the schedule matters as much as the signing

The Ducati deal itself is older news: the announcement came October 29. The new material is Steve Matthes' October 30 Racer X interview with Savatgy. He describes the importance of a domestic schedule and more time with his children, alongside a substantial training and testing block before the season. He plans to remain at Sandbox.

Savatgy's early comments about the motorcycle are measured. He had limited time on it and development still ahead, so treating the signing as proof of an immediate competitive breakthrough would run ahead of his own assessment. The next useful evidence will come from preparation and racing, not the announcement photograph.

What to watch next

First, the finalized Auckland classification and any official revisions. Then, how the US-bound riders translate these offseason outings into their winter preparation. For Ducati, watch the testing progress rather than assuming a new team has already answered its setup questions.

This edition is deliberately shorter: the checked US series and sanctioning-body sources did not yield another newly verified major announcement. Podcast listings and inaccessible videos were not treated as reviewed interviews, and unresolved rider-market speculation is not a confirmed signing.
