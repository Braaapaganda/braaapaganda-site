Braaapaganda Daily: Hicks Seals French SX2 Title as Roczen Sweeps Paris

Michael Hicks tops the French SX2 standings on a Stark, Sacha Coenen wins the open SX2 weekend, and Triumph puts two riders in the SX1 top five.

November 23, 2026

Michael Hicks left Paris seventh in the open SX2 weekend standings and first in the French SX2 championship. Both are true. Sunday settled the two competitions on the same track: Hicks took the season title on his electric Stark, while Sacha Coenen swept the day to become Prince of Paris. In SX1, Ken Roczen finished what he started Saturday: six races, six wins.

Hicks turns a 21-point lead into a 29-point title margin

The French federation’s provisional season table, still subject to ratification, puts Hicks on 124 points. Jules Piètre and Saad Soulimani finished on 95 each, with Piètre listed second. Piètre topped the national classification on both days in Paris, but Hicks finished second each time. That was enough not just to protect the lead he brought into Sunday, but to extend it as the riders behind him changed places. The Sunday French SX Tour sheet shows the order that mattered for the championship.

The bike is part of the story. FFMoto identified Hicks’s Stark as electric before the event. His season shows what that bike and rider accomplished in this championship. It doesn’t tell us how the same package would fare under a different rulebook or race format. In a November 20 MotoOnline interview, Hicks said the lack of a clutch made wheelspin harder to manage on slick surfaces. That’s his account of racing in Australia, not an explanation for the Paris results.

Coenen sweeps Sunday. Roczen goes six for six.

Coenen won both Sunday SX2 sprints and the final. The Prince of Paris weekend sheet puts him ahead of Cole Thompson and Piètre, with Saturday winner Jesson Turner fourth and Hicks seventh. The useful U.S. connection is that KTM named Coenen to its 2027 U.S. 250 lineup last week. Paris gives us a race-day look at how quickly he adapted indoors. It doesn’t tell us whether he can reproduce those starts against an AMA field in January.

Roczen won both Superpoles as well as all six SX1 races. The King of Paris classification places Jason Anderson second and Jordon Smith third. Aaron Plessinger finished fifth in the first race weekend of his Triumph tenure; Jo Shimoda was eighth. For Triumph, the two top-five overalls are the useful benchmark. These were exhibition results, not AMA points.

What the paddock is talking about

Racer X’s Sunday report pointed to the revised whoops as one difference between Coenen’s Saturday and Sunday. That’s an observation from the event, not proof that the track change alone produced the sweep. PulpMX’s post-race interview collection is available if you want the paddock audio alongside the timing sheets.

Next up: Mini O’s at Gatorback

FloRacing’s schedule and timing guide puts Monday Supercross action at 7 a.m. Eastern, with the mains scheduled for Tuesday before motocross practice. Prime Video lists a free Monday stream at 1 p.m. Eastern, a later viewing window, not the start of racing. The classes it will show were not confirmed at cutoff. There’s something to watch beyond the winner: the AMA’s Road to Supercross bulletin awards two licensing points for a top-ten overall in 250 A or 250 Pro Sport on the Supercross track. Check the finals, not the heat sheets, for those finishes.
