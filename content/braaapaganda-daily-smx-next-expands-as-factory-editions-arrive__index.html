Braaapaganda Daily: SMX Next Expands as Factory Editions Arrive

November 4, 2026

The 2027 development ladder is taking clearer shape. A November 3 announcement gives SMX Next a combined Supercross and Pro Motocross season, while new Factory Edition motorcycles offer another offseason talking point. The useful distinction today is between confirmed formats and equipment announcements, and the competitive conclusions that still need racing to support them.

SMX Next: ten races across two disciplines

The SMX League’s November 3 announcement sets out ten regular-season SMX Next events for 2027: five alongside Supercross and five alongside Pro Motocross, with 25-rider fields. The first four rounds in each discipline lead into a Showdown at the fifth.

Points across all ten events will determine postseason seeding. The top 15 riders in combined points automatically qualify for the World All-Stars, with up to three prospects coming through the Australian program and four further riders selected by invitation for a 22-rider postseason field. The World All-Stars are scheduled alongside the first two SMX playoffs.

Analysis: a combined season gives fans more than a single prospect-race result to follow. The next useful checks are the confirmed field and how riders perform across both disciplines; the announcement does not establish who will make a successful professional transition.

Factory Editions: new hardware, claims still to test

KTM’s November 3 launch introduces the 2027 250 SX-F and 450 SX-F Factory Editions. The manufacturer identifies revised frame geometry, WP XACT closed-cartridge spring forks and a redesigned 250 engine among the changes. Its North American release says dealer availability begins in December. Those are announced specifications, not an independent ride verdict.

Husqvarna’s North American announcement, also dated November 3, presents the 2027 FC 250 and FC 450 Factory Editions with a new 250 engine, chassis revisions and closed-cartridge spring forks. It says dealer availability begins in December. Local availability should be confirmed with dealers rather than inferred from another market’s launch.

The shared offseason question is straightforward: how will the announced changes feel under different riders and on different tracks? Manufacturer descriptions can identify what changed; independent testing and racing will have to show what those changes deliver.

Rules context: check the dated bulletin

The AMA’s November 2 bulletin on 2027 Supercross rules is an earlier development, not a new Wednesday announcement. It specifies 20-rider main events, 38 evening qualifiers and 19-rider heats, with the top eight advancing from each heat. It also addresses mandatory chest and back protection. Riders and teams should consult the bulletin and applicable rules directly for their preparation.

The 2027 minimum-age policy of 18 was announced in December 2024; its appearance in the bulletin should not be confused with its first announcement.

On the listening list

The official description for PulpMX Show 684 lists Cooper Webb discussing Supercross preparation on his Husqvarna, alongside guests including Justin Brayton and Phil Nicoletti. The audio was not reviewed for this edition, so this is a listening pointer, not a summary of Webb’s comments or an assessment of his preparation.

Coverage note: Manufacturer performance claims are attributed above. Inaccessible Cooksey and Hopper videos were not reviewed, and no unseen interviews or opinions are summarized.
