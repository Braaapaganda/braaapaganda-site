Braaapaganda Daily: ClubMX Adds Mike Brown to Its Amateur Program

ClubMX details Brown’s coaching role, Shimoda gets another shot at Roczen in Paris, and Mini O’s starts with practice.

November 21, 2026

The latest U.S. move is on the training side, not the starting gate. ClubMX has added Mike Brown to its staff, with amateur riders as his main assignment. Meanwhile, Paris race day gives us another look at Jo Shimoda against Ken Roczen on a 450, and the first Mini O’s gates drop Sunday in Florida.

ClubMX adds Brown where riders learn to race

Racer X published the ClubMX release November 20. The same team statement, carried by Vurbmoto November 19, says the 2001 AMA 125cc national champion will work primarily with amateurs on technique, racecraft and preparation. That’s a coaching hire at the South Carolina facility, not a change to the ClubMX pro lineup.

There’s a useful distinction here: getting faster in practice and putting together a race are different jobs. Brown’s remit includes both, and success won’t show up in one stopwatch number. Watch whether the riders working with him can turn good starts into clean opening laps and get through traffic without giving away the pace they had in qualifying.

Media watch: The hire didn’t come out of nowhere Friday. In Racerhead on November 13, Racer X reported that ClubMX had already announced Brown’s arrival during the PulpMX show from the facility. The written release explains where he’ll spend his time. The archive is useful context, not a second announcement.

Paris gives Shimoda a second shot at Roczen

Paris is not Shimoda’s 450 Supercross debut. At the November 7 AUSX Open in Geelong, Roczen swept all three finals, Christian Craig ran second in all three, and Shimoda took third overall with three fourths. A week later, Shimoda won a Perth heat and put early pressure on Craig in the main. Roczen wasn’t at Perth, so Geelong is the useful head-to-head baseline.

Shimoda has already won a premier-class outdoor overall on a Honda 450: 2–1–1 at Saitama in September 2024. The question today isn’t whether he can ride the bike. It’s whether he can start close enough to make Roczen race him, then stay there in the longer final. The French federation’s format preview says that final is worth double in the Paris event scoring. This is an offseason matchup, not an AMA points race.

What to watch next

Saturday: The Paris venue lists a 1 p.m. Eastern show, with Sunday at 8 a.m. Eastern. MXGP confirms its live stream. For the separate French SX2 title fight, use the Hicks preview and watch the season standings rather than inferring a title result from the Paris podium.

Sunday: Today’s Mini O’s program is practice. Unlimited Sports lists the Gatorback schedule and practice orders, and FloRacing starts its race coverage Sunday at 7 a.m. Eastern with sight laps ahead of the Supercross program. For the younger riders, the first gate drops matter more than the practice sheet.
