Braaapaganda Daily: Hicks Brings the French SX2 Lead to Paris

An American leads France’s SX2 championship on a Stark, Plessinger is entered for his Triumph debut, and the Turner report puts last-chance qualifying back in focus.

November 20, 2026

Paris has two SX2 stories on the same gate. One is the international weekend result. The other is a French championship that an American leads on an electric bike. The French federation’s November 19 preview makes that second race the one to follow closely: Michael Hicks brings a 17-point advantage to Paris, but the title is still open.

Hicks’s 17-point lead is in the French championship

The French federation’s preview has Hicks at 80, Saad Soulimani at 63 and Mickaël Lamarque at 54. The top ten in the French standings are joined in SX2 by wild cards including Sacha Coenen, Henry Miller and Cole Thompson. That gives Hicks two sets of rivals to worry about: the riders fighting him for position on the track and the French title contenders who can close his points lead. A rider can feature in the Paris race result without being in the season-long title fight.

What to watch: Check the federation’s Paris timing page for the races and its French SX Tour results for the championship. Don’t read a clinch off the international finish alone. In SX1, Aaron Plessinger is entered on the Triumph for the competitive debut the team announced September 30. It’s a first look at him racing the bike in traffic, not a prediction of where he’ll finish in January.

What changes at tech when the bike is electric

The French federation’s July 27 amendment sets a 117 kg minimum and a 44 kW battery-output limit for electric SX2 machines, with a narrow allowance for brief spikes. That figure is measured at the battery, not the rear wheel. Riders must supply their own paddock power and present a specified battery extinguisher and an electric-vehicle fire blanket at inspection.

That’s a practical difference, not just an argument about whether electric bikes are faster. The FIM said November 18 that more than 110 officials and federation representatives took part in two electric-motorcycle safety sessions on November 9–10. It plans a dedicated technical qualification course in early 2027. That announcement says nothing about who is working Paris or what Hicks’s bike actually recorded. The 2027 AMA pro Supercross rulebook still specifies single-cylinder displacement classes and petroleum-based fuel. The French rules aren’t a change to who can enter an American pro round.

Media watch: why a last-chance race matters

In a November 20 MotoOnline report, Alex Gobert revisits American rider Lux Turner’s scoreless November 14 Perth round. Gobert reports that Turner’s bike problem ended his heat and the scheduled SX2 program had no last-chance qualifier. The promoter’s standings confirm Turner is fifth, 36 points behind Cameron McAdoo. Nobody can say Turner would have transferred through an LCQ. The useful question for the series is whether riders can tell, before a round, exactly when they’ll get that second route into the main. AUSX wraps in Newcastle November 28.
