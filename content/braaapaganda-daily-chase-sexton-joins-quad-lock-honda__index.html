Braaapaganda Daily: Chase Sexton Joins Quad Lock Honda

The Honda return is official. Kevin Windham joins Yarrive Konsky, and the team plans to split title sponsorship across the season.

November 28, 2026

Chase Sexton’s 2027 ride is no longer a question. Quad Lock Honda announced Friday that the former 450 Supercross and Pro Motocross champion will race its CRF450R in the 2027 SMX season. The move puts him back on the brand he won the 2023 Supercross title with, but not in the same factory team. It also brings Kevin Windham into the program and gives the team a different way to sell sponsorship across the season.

Sexton returns to Honda on an independent team

The November 27 team announcement, published by Racer X, confirms Sexton on the new CRF450R alongside Christian Craig, with Dean Wilson slated for selected rounds. American Honda racing manager Brandon Wilson backed the partnership in the release. That makes this a Honda-supported effort run by Yarrive Konsky’s team, not a return to Team Honda HRC. MotoOnline’s report describes the plan as spanning Supercross, the outdoor Nationals and the SMX postseason.

Windham’s part in the deal goes beyond being a familiar face in the pits. The team says he will work with Sexton and help build its amateur-to-pro program. In the announcement, Windham says he’d been talking to American Honda about an amateur program and approached Konsky at Pala about pooling their efforts. Sexton cited his conversations with Windham as a reason for choosing the team. That development ambition has some immediate names attached: Cameron McAdoo, Michael Mosiman and Brodie Connolly in the 250 program, Charli Cannon in WMX and Dylan Rempel at the amateur level. Those are the roster as announced, not fresh signings for every rider listed.

Quad Lock stays, but the lead sponsor can change by race

One detail easy to miss in the rider announcement: Quad Lock returns for a third year, and Konsky says the team plans to split up its title sponsorship rights across the 31-event calendar. The pitch is that a sponsor can pick the markets or times of year it wants instead of buying the same exposure at every stop. That could matter for an independent team trying to fund a 450 title bid and a development program at once. The announcement didn’t attach dollar figures or a round-by-round sponsor list, so it doesn’t tell us who is paying for what.

In the media: In his reporting alongside the release, Racer X’s Jason Weigandt raised the possibility that Sexton could start the season on a more production-based setup while parts for the redesigned Honda are developed. That is a reported possibility, not a confirmed Anaheim spec. Racer X also posted its Sexton interview. At Anaheim on January 9, the first useful comparison will be how the bike comes out of the gate and how repeatable Sexton’s starts are across the night. The badge and the support are confirmed. The race setup and its results aren’t.

Newcastle: Clout turns 44 points around, McAdoo holds on

The wet finale produced different stories for Sexton’s new teammates. Christian Craig arrived 44 points up in the five-event AUSX table but did not start either SX1 final. MX Large reported that Craig went out in his heat and there was no last-chance qualifier. Luke Clout went 1–2 for 47 points. That gets him to 210, three clear of Craig on the five-event math, and Computime’s provisional four-round Australian standings have Clout ahead 170–157. Jed Beaton went 2–1 and is listed first on the provisional round sheet, level with Clout on 47. Clout got the titles, Beaton got the night.

Cameron McAdoo went eighth in both SX2 finals, enough for both titles on the posted scores. The provisional Australian table has him at 172 to Max Anstie’s 161. Adding Newcastle to the five-event table gives McAdoo 200 to Anstie’s 186. Anstie’s 2–3 led the round sheet, even though Brodie Connolly and Ryder Kingsford won the individual finals. MX Vice also reports the title outcomes and the two round winners. Friday’s preview showed how a split could happen; in the posted results, the same riders top both sets of points.

What to watch at Gatorback

At Mini O’s, Racer X’s Friday recap lists Kane Bollasina, Landon Gibson and Grant McDonald as the 250 Pro Sport, 250 A and 450 Pro Sport Supercross champions, respectively, matching Vurbmoto’s published results. Those are the Supercross results from earlier in the week, not Saturday’s outdoor winners. The final motocross day at Gatorback is Saturday; the organizer scores the combined Olympiad separately.
