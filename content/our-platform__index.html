Braaapaganda proposals / For discussion & adoption

Our Platform

A stronger sport. A fairer deal for the people who ride.

This is what we are advocating for, not current AMA policy. These proposals are separate from the facts in our AMA election guide. They do not create a live assistance program, petition process or new enforcement powers.

Six foundations for reform

Member-led priorities. Clear accountability.

  • 01

    Transparent elections

    Publish dates, qualifications, the process and results in one easy-to-find place.

  • 02

    Rider & privateer voice

    Give active riders and independent teams a meaningful voice in decisions that shape their sport.

  • 03

    Fair enforcement

    Clear rules, consistent treatment, reasoned decisions and a fair chance to respond.

  • 04

    Visible conflicts

    Publish relevant interests. Require recusals when a decision-maker has a conflict.

  • 05

    Financial accountability

    Show where member money goes, disclose funding sources and report independently audited spending.

  • 06

    Stronger grassroots

    Make cost, safety and access central to decisions affecting local racing and everyday riders.

Flagship proposal / Rider care

No Rider Left Behind

The sport’s responsibility should not end when an injured rider leaves the track.

A proposed injury navigator, funded recovery and family support, independent serious-incident learning, and ongoing follow-through with the rider’s consent.

The AMA should seek out and work with established support organizations. Coordinate referrals with permission, respect partners’ eligibility and funding limits, and address gaps instead of duplicating effective programs.

Read the No Rider Left Behind proposal

Care must not depend on publicity or filing a complaint. This is a proposal, not an operating assistance program, an existing partnership or a guarantee of insurance payments or compensation.

Where Are They Now?

Injured riders can request a conversation with the host about life after the injury, in their own words and on their own terms.

No paid membership required. A request is not a commitment to appear or share medical details, and does not guarantee an episode. Private contact and public story-sharing options are separate from the proposed care program.

The safeguards matter

Who would control the process?

An outside integrity provider, protected funding and a seven-seat oversight board: four independent experts, two representatives elected by verified AMA individual members and one rider-elected representative with privateer experience. Oversight protects appointments, budgets and service quality; it does not choose case outcomes. Investigators, disciplinary decision-makers and appeal reviewers must be separate. Funders, promoters, manufacturers and petitioners cannot direct an outcome.

How would the 100-member trigger work?

After an objective scope and completeness check, one specific review question gets a 14-calendar-day support window. Each current AMA individual member may endorse it once, with private verification and duplicate checks. At 100 valid endorsements, the substantive preliminary inquiry is compulsory, without a second test requiring members to prove wrongdoing. No additional petition fee, race license, minimum membership tenure or Braaapaganda subscription is proposed. There would be no public signer list.

Would a petition change race results or decide guilt?

No. It requests an inquiry, not a penalty. It does not suspend racing, change points, make results provisional or extend existing protest and appeal deadlines. The evidence may explain lawful conduct, establish no breach, remain inconclusive, justify a separate disciplinary referral or reveal a rule gap. Only properly authorized procedures can impose consequences. The lead member could separately challenge a failure to deliver the promised process.

Why 100 members and 14 days?

These are explicit pilot choices, not proven sporting standards or a measure of how representative a complaint is. Test access, duplicate verification, case volume, fairness and cost in a 90-day simulation using synthetic or lawfully consented historical scenarios. Independent evaluation should inform any revision before launch. Supporter numbers never add evidentiary weight.

Build the authority before launching the promise.

Implementation needs AMA adoption of the relevant powers and rules, independent appointments, protected funding, and enforceable cooperation, records-access and privacy agreements. No Rider Left Behind also needs its own funded operating plan. This page does not establish those programs or collect live petitions.

Our reform platform / Proposed 2027 media charter

Independent media.
Accountable promoters.

Fair access to report. Freedom to ask hard questions. A fair hearing when access is taken away.

This is a proposal, not an existing guarantee. We are advocating for an AMA Media Independence and Access Charter. The appeal and petition routes below would require formal adoption and binding agreements. This page does not accept appeals or signatures.

Start with AMA Supercross. Pro Motocross, other SMX events and other series would need separate adoption by the organizations with authority. The complete proposed 2027 credential agreement and fee schedule have not been verified.

Four protections worth writing into the rules

  • 01

    Fair creator access

    One application portal. Published criteria. A real route for freelancers and small creators, judged on their work and safety readiness, not just audience size or sponsor connections.

  • 02

    Protected reporting & rider sources

    No retaliation for critical coverage, hard questions or good-faith rider interviews. Reporters and sources need a review route outside the accused manager’s chain of command.

  • 03

    Visible ownership & conflicts

    Disclose controlling interests and relevant promoter, sponsor and broadcast ties. Keep conflicted people out of coverage and credential decisions. Protect independent editors’ final say.

  • 04

    Clear media rights

    Publish terms for original interviews, paddock reporting and photos, plus fair editorial licenses for race clips. Explain costs and reuse. No invented “free seconds” rule.

Two routes. No signatures needed to appeal your own ban.

An individual access decision and a systemic promoter problem need different ways to be heard.

Route A / Individual access appeal

Your access. Your appeal.

Anyone denied, suspended or banned by a participating promoter from event attendance, a press pass or paddock access could take that decision to an AMA-established independent panel.

Under adopted binding agreements, the panel could uphold, shorten, modify or reverse the restriction. Promoters and competing outlets could give evidence, but could not decide or veto the appeal.

Route B / Systemic promoter accountability

A pattern. A required answer.

A petition on an issue within the adopted process’s remit would trigger a mandatory inquiry and a reasoned written promoter response.

100members in14calendar days

Each signer must be a unique, verified current AMA individual member. This nationwide threshold is a proposed pilot choice, not an established benchmark.

An inquiry and a written answer, not a finding of guilt

Signatures would not prove wrongdoing, impose a penalty or automatically cancel a race. An individual access appeal would never depend on this petition.

Safety still comes first. A genuine immediate safety risk can justify immediate removal, followed by prompt review. An appeal is not a promise of reinstatement and cannot override court orders or lawful venue restrictions beyond the process’s legal and contractual authority.

What would this look like?

Three fictional “what if” examples. They illustrate the proposed process, not claims about any real person, outlet or incident.

  1. What if / 01
    A rider speaks up

    A hard question after a hard race

    A rider questions the medical response in an interview. Later, the rider claims a promoter reduced their paddock access because they spoke up.

    Under this proposal

    The rider could appeal the access restriction without collecting signatures. Independent reviewers would hear both sides and examine whether retaliation occurred.

    The claim is not proof. Good-faith criticism is protected; the evidence still has to be tested.

  2. What if / 02
    A pass is suspended

    The gate closes on a reporter

    An independent reporter learns their press pass has been suspended. They do not know which rule they supposedly broke or how long the restriction lasts.

    Under this proposal

    The promoter would have to give written reasons, the evidence, the rule, the duration and an appeal path. A no-fee independent panel would review it, before the event where practicable.

    Small outlet or large, the review should turn on the rules and evidence, not whether coverage was flattering.

  3. What if / 03
    Members see a pattern

    Similar complaints deserve an answer

    Members hear similar access complaints from several creators. They want to understand how the promoter makes those decisions.

    Under this proposal

    100 verified current AMA individual members support one in-scope question within 14 calendar days. That triggers an inquiry, examination of relevant lawfully available records and a written promoter response.

    The response might explain a lawful decision, reveal a rule gap or support a finding of breach. Signatures do not decide which.

Start with answers. Escalate only on evidence.

Proposed remedies would have to be written into enforceable agreements. Each step needs a reasoned, properly authorized decision, not a popularity vote.

  1. 1

    Written response

    Require the promoter to answer the specific concern and supply relevant records through lawful, agreed access.

  2. 2

    Independent review

    Check conflicts, test the evidence, hear the response and publish privacy-protected findings.

  3. 3

    Corrective order

    Where authorized, require a specific fix, a reasonable cure period and proof of compliance.

  4. 4

    Proportionate penalties

    Publish established noncompliance and use only contractual penalties that the adopted framework authorizes.

  5. 5

    Last-resort sanction action

    Consider suspension, nonrenewal or withdrawal of AMA sanction only within legal and contractual authority.

A petition cannot cancel a race.

Event-affecting action would be a last resort for serious, repeated material noncompliance that lesser remedies cannot adequately resolve. Withdrawing AMA sanction is not itself a legal power to cancel an event. Any postponement or cancellation must be expressly authorized by the governing agreements and law.

  • Independent findings
  • Notice
  • Meaningful chance to cure
  • Hearing
  • Appeal opportunity
  • Authorized board decision

No current unilateral AMA cancellation power is assumed. The relevant promoter agreements, including the unreviewed Supercross agreement, require legal review.

Protect the people who did not cause the breach.

Before an event-affecting measure, require a funded plan for riders’ entries and championship treatment, workers, ticket refunds, notice and travel disruption. Name the responsible parties. Prefer effective remedies that avoid unnecessary disruption, and publish reinstatement conditions and review dates.

The details behind the graphics

Open a topic for the proposed safeguards, timelines and source limits.

How would fair credentials and ownership safeguards work?

Keep one reusable application portal with published requirements, status tracking and a named decision-maker. Separate basic reporting, restricted trackside access, broadcast production and commercial filming. Apply equal safety and capacity criteria, publish how scarce spaces are allocated and let applicants correct missing information.

Freelancers and small creators could qualify through original-work samples, credible assignments or self-directed reporting plans, contact details and safety training. Audience size, platform, employment status or lack of series sponsorship would not alone disqualify journalism. Basic editorial access would not require content approval, favorable coverage, anti-criticism promises or promotional output.

Outlets would disclose controlling interests and relevant promoter, sanctioning, sponsor and paid-broadcast relationships, including on affected coverage. Conflicted people could not approve, suppress or edit that reporting. Independent editors would retain protected final authority. Credential decisions would be separate from competing outlets and promoter commercial departments. Disclosure failures would receive independent, proportionate review, not become a pretext to exclude critics. This first version proposes binding safeguards, not mandatory ownership divestiture.

What would protect reporters, riders and their sources?

Criticism, investigations, difficult questions and refusal to provide favorable coverage would not justify reduced access or revocation. Define unsafe behavior, harassment and credential misuse narrowly. Protect good-faith rider interviews with any qualified journalist, including critical outlets, and good-faith safety or governance concerns.

Add an express protection to the AMA’s disparagement provision and relevant contracts. Confidential complaints would be reviewed outside the accused manager’s reporting chain. Preserve proportionate, legitimate confidentiality duties without promising absolute anonymity or blanket whistleblower immunity.

An access restriction would require written notice of the conduct, evidence, applicable rule, duration, scope and appeal route. An independent roster would be selected transparently, with journalism, rider-welfare and governance expertise. Promoters and competing outlets could submit evidence but could not adjudicate or veto appeals.

Preserve existing AMA disciplinary appeals while creating this distinct promoter-access route. Adopted agreements would require promoters to honor panel outcomes, with venue participation where needed. Consider temporary reinstatement or limited access while urgent cases proceed. Genuine immediate safety risks permit immediate removal followed by prompt review, subject to lawful court and venue limits.

What could journalists publish or license?

Publish clear permissions for journalists’ original interviews, paddock reporting and photography, alongside editorial licenses for short race clips used in news or analysis. State permitted duration, platforms, timing, attribution, prices and reuse. Preserve legitimate live-broadcast exclusivity while negotiating proportionate, equally available editorial licenses.

Classify the actual assignment and use, including brand commissioning and editorial control. Advertising or sponsorship revenue alone would not turn journalism into commercial brand production. Announce ordinary rights changes prospectively, without retroactively restricting authorized work.

No guaranteed number of free seconds. Copyright and fair use remain fact-specific. Contractual permissions and statutory rights are different. An editorial license would not replace rights that the law independently provides.

What are the proposed deadlines and petition rules?

These are proposed service targets, not current guarantees. Pilot them with adequate independent staffing and report justified extensions.

  • 2 business days: acknowledge a qualifying petition or receipt of an individual appeal.
  • 10 business days: require the promoter’s reasoned petition response.
  • 20 business days after that response: target independent findings; give reasons and a revised date when an extension is necessary.
  • 30 calendar days: allow cure of established noncompliance, unless a decision sets a justified alternative.
  • 10 business days: allow an appeal of findings or remedies.
  • Urgent individual access cases: target a determination within 2 business days and before the affected event where practicable.

The petition pilot requires 100 unique, privately verified current AMA individual members within 14 calendar days. No geographic connection, affected-member status, five-year tenure or additional subscription would be required. Do not publish a signer list. The threshold triggers an inquiry and written response within the adopted remit, never an evidentiary presumption or automatic sanction.

What must be in place before any of this starts?

Verify authority across the AMA, AMA Pro Racing, promoters, venues and broadcasters, then incorporate the charter into the agreements that actually govern the relevant events. Do not assume the AMA controls AMA Pro Racing or every series agreement. Start with AMA Supercross; expansion requires separate adoption.

Specify remedies, enforcement powers and decision-makers in advance. Fund administration and independent review through ring-fenced organizational contributions, without donor editorial control or prohibitive applicant fees. Pilot for 90 days, test staffing and deadlines, and give 30 calendar days’ notice of ordinary policy changes.

Publish aggregate credential decisions, delays, appeals, petition outcomes and compliance remedies while protecting personal information and sources. Keep genuine safety-emergency powers separate from media enforcement, with prompt reasons and review. Do not punish riders, reporters or members for a promoter’s breach.

Primary sources: what is verified, and what is not?

Source context, not evidence that this charter is already in force. The complete proposed 2027 credential agreement, incorporated documents, fee schedule and applicable private promoter agreements remain unverified. The sources below support the issues and comparisons described; they do not establish a particular act of retaliation or improper reporting.

  1. 2026 AMA Supercross Rulebook: section 1.4.4(a)(1), printed page 22, permits fines for specified disparagement by credentialed participants. That is not proof that all journalists are fined or that any rider was punished for an interview. Sections 1.2.12(c) and 3.1.3(g) provide specified revocation and penalty appeals. Section 1.5.8 contains broad recording-rights provisions with an express fair-use or AMA-policy exception. These existing rules are distinct from this proposal.
  2. SMX and Pro Motocross public media-access guidance: public eligibility and assignment restrictions, not the complete proposed 2027 credential agreement.
  3. Racer X’s June 2025 masthead and operator agreement: identify editorial leadership and the operator, but do not establish current beneficial ownership percentages.
  4. Official 2025 Pro Motocross media guide, printed page 2, and Feld’s SMX partnership announcement: document promoter leadership and the partnership. Overlapping roles justify examining conflicts, not assuming misconduct.
  5. Rugby World Cup 2023 media accreditation terms, section 1.5: an editorial-independence precedent, not a guarantee of unrestricted access.
  6. North Carolina A&T media credential policy: allows denial and revocation appeals outside the initial athletics decision-maker but still inside the university. A fully independent panel would go further.
  7. U.S. Copyright Office fair-use guidance: explains case-specific analysis and the absence of a fixed permissible amount.
  8. AMA general sanction terms and conditions: do not establish a right to override the unreviewed Supercross promoter agreement. No express Supercross event-cancellation power for media-policy misconduct was identified in the reviewed rules. Participant-license revocation and withdrawal of an event sanction are different actions.

The goal: keep the gate fair, the reporting independent and the decisions accountable. Build the legal authority and independent process before promising the remedy.
