Braaapaganda Daily: Cochran Joins Star as Yamaha Expands Its Rider Pipeline

October 27, 2026

October 27, 2026 | Morning edition

Yamaha supplied the clearest change in the American motocross picture after yesterday’s morning edition: Casey Cochran is joining Star Racing, and three more riders are entering its amateur program. Those are announced deals, not another round of offseason speculation.

Today’s shorter roundup focuses on those moves and a useful piece of bike-testing commentary. A busy rumor cycle does not require us to manufacture a busy news day.

Confirmed: Cochran completes Star’s announced 250 lineup

In an October 26 team announcement carried by the official Supercross website, Monster Energy Yamaha Star Racing confirmed a multi-year agreement with Casey Cochran for the 2027 SMX season. The announced six-rider 250 group comprises Cochran, Cole Davies, Kayden Minear, Caden Dudney, Landen Gordon and Pierce Brown.

The release identifies injuries as a limiting factor in Cochran’s recent professional seasons. Team manager Jensen Hendler expressed confidence in the combination of Cochran’s ability, work ethic and the team’s program; Cochran described the opportunity as one he had worked toward. Those are the participants’ expectations, not a guarantee of results.

Why it matters: another important 250 seat is now settled. The next useful information will be how the team assigns riders and prepares them for competition, rather than treating a signing announcement as a prediction of the standings.

Source: Yamaha team announcement reproduced by Supercross Live, October 26.

Confirmed: three additions to the amateur program

A separate Yamaha announcement on October 26 added Levi Townley, Jaggar Townley and Easton Graves on multi-year agreements. They join Carson Wood in Star’s amateur program.

The announced plans put Levi in 250A, Open Pro Sport and SMX Next; Jaggar in 125 B/C and Schoolboy 1; and Graves in 250B and SMX Next. The team named Mini O’s at Gatorback, November 21–28, as its next amateur-season starting point.

Our read: viewed together, the announcements show investment at more than one stage of a rider’s development. The professional roster is the immediate story; the amateur additions are the longer-term one. That distinction matters: an amateur agreement should not be presented as an immediate full-time professional seat.

Source: Yamaha’s amateur-team release carried by Racer X, October 26.

Media conversation: bike feel is not a horsepower contest

Kris Keefer’s October 26 written comparison on PulpMX examines the 2027 Honda CRF450R and Yamaha YZ450F. His assessment favors the Honda for low-throttle connection, front-tire feedback and room to move; he credits the Yamaha with a livelier initial response and greater suspension comfort.

These are attributed test impressions of production motorcycles. They are not evidence that a particular factory rider’s race results were caused by a stock bike’s characteristics. The useful takeaway is narrower: riders can value different combinations of response, feedback and comfort, so a single “best bike” label can conceal the actual trade-offs.

Source: Kris Keefer’s Honda–Yamaha comparison, October 26.

What to watch next

Watch for specific competition assignments and further team announcements, then evidence from riding and racing. Keep the amateur class plans separate from professional championship expectations. And when evaluating offseason bike discussion, ask whether the speaker is describing a production test, a factory setup or an individual preference.
